**Slim-shielding verdict after the 230V discovery (2026-09-11):** operator asked whether slim Cat6a shields properly or the bending should be accepted. Answer: **slim SFTP shields adequately even next to the 230V run — the foil intercepts electric-field (capacitive) coupling regardless of conductor gauge; slim loses only the braid, a minor loss at this EMI level — and NEVER "accept the bending": one kink permanently deforms pair geometry and hand-manufactures the marginal-link failure mode.** Final ranking: (1) slim 28AWG SFTP correct length = pragmatic winner; (2) full-size Cat6a/7 SFTP + right-angle plug at the bend end = max margin; (3) kinked cable = never. Two footnotes: slim "Cat6a" labels are optimistic (really Cat6-class at short lengths — irrelevant at 1G/few meters, would only matter for a future 2.5/10G run), and 28AWG is officially PoE-de-rated — harmless here since this cable carries the primary's PoE feed at only ~5 W over a few meters (only 30W+/15m+ would make slim wrong). **Concrete spec settled (2026-09-11): AWG 27/7** — 7-strand stranded 27AWG slim, the sweet spot between 28AWG (tougher at the fragile terminations) and full-size (bend radius ~15–20 mm, effectively unkinkable for the bend-heavy run); same footnotes apply (optimistic Cat6a label irrelevant at 1G/few m; PoE drop negligible at ~5 W). Buying caveat given: slim cords ship in UTP AND shielded versions and listing titles can't be trusted — verify the product is the foil/F-UTP variant given the 230V parallel run; add a velcro strain-relief loop or right-angle plug if the tight bend sits right at a plug.
